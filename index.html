nimmesh

Pay with NIM when there is no internet

Sign a transaction on a phone with no connection at all. It hops device to device over Bluetooth until it reaches someone who has a connection, and they broadcast it for you.

Install on iPhone

Open in Safari. Installs on registered devices.

139

bytes, and that is the whole idea

A signed Nimiq transaction is a self contained blob that carries its own proof and stays valid for about two hours. The network does not care how those bytes arrived, only that they are valid and on time. So the internet is needed at the final hop, and every hop before it can be a phone.

How it travels

01

Sign with no signal

Your phone builds and signs the transaction on device. Nothing leaves the secure enclave except bytes that are safe to broadcast.

02

Hop through strangers

Nearby phones carry it onward without ever reading it. A relay cannot tell a payment from noise, and does not need to.

03

Someone has a connection

The first device with data broadcasts it to the Nimiq network, then a receipt travels back the same way and your screen settles.

What is not proven yet. Cross chain swaps between NIM and USDC run end to end and settle, but on test networks. The mainnet phone to phone swap has not been run, and this page will say so until it has.

Further than a room

Bluetooth reaches about thirty metres. The same 139 bytes fit inside a single LoRa packet and a single text message, which is where this goes next.

Onto the LoRa mesh

A signed transaction fits in one Meshtastic packet with room to spare, so it needs no splitting and cannot half arrive. Community LoRa networks already cover whole cities, and a radio costs about the same as dinner.

Down to a text message

One binary SMS carries exactly 140 bytes. A basic transfer is 139. For anywhere with a bar of signal and no data at all, that is the entire payment in a single message.
